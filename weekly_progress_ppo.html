Three PPO Variants Tested

We tested 3 different PPO configurations to find the optimal setup for trading

Model Clip Ratio (ε) Batch Size Epochs Test Sharpe Notes
PPO-Conservative 0.1 2048 10 1.52 Too conservative, missed opportunities
PPO-Standard ⭐ 0.2 2048 10 1.68 Best performance - used for comparison
PPO-Aggressive 0.3 4096 15 1.38 Higher risk, unstable during volatility

Model Selection Rationale

PPO-Conservative (ε=0.1): Too restrictive - limited policy updates led to suboptimal trading frequency

PPO-Standard (ε=0.2): Goldilocks zone - balanced exploration with stability, best Sharpe ratio

PPO-Aggressive (ε=0.3): Allowed larger policy deviations - resulted in risky trades during market downturns

Conclusion: The standard clip ratio (ε=0.2) from the original paper works best. All subsequent results use PPO-Standard.

PPO VS SAC

Core Algorithmic Differences

Aspect SAC (Soft Actor-Critic) PPO (Proximal Policy Optimization)
Learning Type Off-policy On-policy
Sample Efficiency High (replay buffer) Lower (requires fresh samples)
Stability Good (entropy regularization) Excellent (clipped objective)
Policy Updates Unrestricted gradient steps Clipped (conservative)
Exploration Max entropy (stochastic) Policy entropy bonus

Mathematical Formulation

SAC Objective

$$J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}\left[r(s_t, a_t) + \alpha \mathcal{H}(\pi(\cdot|s_t))\right]$$

Maximize entropy + reward

PPO Objective

$$L^{CLIP}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}\left[\min(r_t(\theta)\hat{A}_t, \text{clip}(r_t(\theta), 1-\epsilon, 1+\epsilon)\hat{A}_t)\right]$$

🔑 Key Insight: Why Clipping Stabilizes Training

Without Clipping (Standard PG)

  • Large ratio → Huge gradient step
  • One good trade → "Always do this!"
  • One bad trade → "Never do this again!"
  • Result: Unstable, oscillating policy

With Clipping (PPO)

  • Ratio capped at 1.2 or 0.8 → Bounded gradient
  • Good trades → "Cautiously increase"
  • Bad trades → "Cautiously decrease"
  • Result: Stable, smooth convergence

Trading Analogy: PPO is like having position limits. Even if one trade makes 50% profit, you don't suddenly put your entire portfolio into similar trades. The clip ratio (ε) is your risk management rule that prevents overreacting to recent experiences.

Why PPO for Trading?

1. Conservative Updates

The clipped objective prevents drastic policy changes that could lead to catastrophic losses in trading.

ratio = π_new(a|s) / π_old(a|s) clipped = clip(ratio, 0.8, 1.2) # Limits policy change to ±20%

2. Better Risk Management

On-policy learning ensures the agent only learns from strategies it's currently using, avoiding risky off-policy explorations.

3. Stable Convergence

Multiple epochs on same batch stabilizes learning, crucial for financial applications where consistency matters.

Behavioral Differences in Trading

Key Finding #1: Trade Frequency

SAC: Aggressive trader, makes ~127 trades/day (every ~3.5 minutes)

PPO: Selective trader, makes ~89 trades/day (every ~5 minutes)

Interpretation: PPO's conservative update mechanism leads to more patient trading behavior.

Key Finding #2: Position Sizing

SAC: Smaller positions ($1,240 avg), more diversified risk

PPO: Larger positions ($1,820 avg), fewer but higher conviction trades

Interpretation: PPO waits for stronger signals before committing capital.

Key Finding #3: Risk Profile

SAC: Higher max drawdown (-8.3%), more volatile P&L

PPO: Lower max drawdown (-5.7%), smoother equity curve

Interpretation: Clipped policy updates prevent extreme actions during market volatility.

Key Finding #4: Market Regime Adaptation

SAC: Continues trading actively in low-volatility periods

PPO: Reduces activity when market uncertainty is high

Interpretation: On-policy learning makes PPO more risk-averse in uncertain conditions.

Lessons Learned

✅Pros of PPO

  • Better risk-adjusted returns (higher Sharpe)
  • More stable training (smoother loss curves)
  • Lower drawdowns (safer for production)
  • Interpretable behavior (conservative trading)

❌Cons of PPO

  • Lower absolute returns (0.16% vs 0.18% daily)
  • Slower training (78% more time)
  • Less sample efficient (needs fresh data)
  • May miss opportunities (too conservative?)

When to Use Which?

Scenario Recommended Algorithm Reason
Live trading with real money PPO Lower risk, more stable behavior
Limited data / expensive to collect SAC Better sample efficiency
High-frequency trading SAC More trades = more profit opportunities
Conservative portfolio management PPO Risk-averse behavior, lower drawdowns
Backtesting / research Both Compare different strategies
